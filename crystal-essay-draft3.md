# Crystal's Draft 3 (her own words, unedited)

Every Friday night, my parents, my sister and I went to have dinner at a family friend's house. One of their grown sons had designs inked down his arms. Id been taught to think that people who had a tattoo were sinners. Every dinner I kept my distance from him, as I'd crouch down beside the chair where my mom sat, I'd peek between the space where her arm met her rib cage. I'd just stare at his tattoos, judging not only them, but him as I decided he was a bad person.
Scrolling late one night, I stopped at a photo of a girl with long black hair down to her waist, like mine, and a fine-line word on her collarbone. I couldn't make out what it said. That's what I liked about it, only she knew. It felt like seeing myself in her, like she was what I'd always wanted to be. I just hadn't known what it was until then. I admired the confidence of doing something for yourself and not for the judgment of others.
While in Montreal for my 17th birthday, my mom gifted me with a tattoo. As we found the nearest cafe to escape the piercing winds and powdery snow, I didn't know if my struggle to find the exact words to explain to her the meaning behind why I decided on "Honesty is Faith" on my lower abdomen, was due to my teeth chattering or because I just didn't know want to say. When I'm asked what my tattoo says, I stutter, toggling back-and-forth between whether or not I want to attempt to explain it or just say "It's just something that I always tell myself." I always land on the second option. He never owed me an explanation for his. I don't owe anyone one for mine.It comes from my favorite Bible story, where a father begs Jesus to heal his son and admits, "I believe; help my unbelief." He's healed anyway. To me, it means being honest with God, even about doubt. After my own health struggles, that's personal. And outside of faith, honesty is the one thing I expect from everyone. Being honest with myself has never been easy and I've realized that I don't need to have everything figured out or understand everything that bothers me, just as this tattoo doesn't have an unambiguous boundary.

Now I actively seek out tattoos on people. My APCSP teacher had one on her back. As I stared at it in class, I kept on wondering what they mean to her. It made sense to me that she had them; she was whimsical and let out a little giggle anytime she saw a cartoon animal. I don't know the significance behind her tattoos and it's important that I don't. Following the winter recess, I entered the classroom with my new tattoo, and she noticed it. She didn't ask what it meant, and I felt relieved. She understood.

Every time I look at myself in the mirror, or look down, it reminds me of what my mom understood before I did: It's not about finding myself. It's about learning how others see the world through their own lens, and finding myself along the way. My mom would never get one herself, but she didn't let her own values get in the way of mine.

I felt alone for the longest time thinking that I was the only one who didn't have myself figured out. But where I stand in the world isn't something that I want to master anymore. Just like I changed my mind about tattoos in the past years, I could wake up in 10 years with a fully developed frontal lobe and think "This is just not what I believe anymore" and I'm okay with not knowing if I will or not.
