# Crystal's Draft 1 (her own words, unedited)

I grew up being taught that tattoos were a sin and I held that pessimistic view of them for the majority of my life. When I grew into my own beliefs and perspectives on my religion, my stance did a 180.

Every Friday night, my parents, my sister and I went to have dinner at a family friend's house. The family was large, the parents were older than mine by about 20 years, they had three adult children, and one of them had designs on his arms. I held a pessimistic view of him because I have been taught to think that people who had a tattoo were sinners and that they'd be going to hell. But every dinner I kept my distance from him, as I'd crouch down beside the chair where my mom sat, I'd peek between the space where her arm met her rib cage. I'd just stare at his tattoos, judging not only them, but him as I decided he was a bad person.

My mom gifted me with a tattoo earlier this year, I struggled to find the exact words to explain to her the meaning behind why I decided on "Honesty is Faith" on my lower abdomen. I write an essay attempting to describe what it means to me, but no one will truly understand why it means that to me. When I'm asked what my tattoo says, I stutter, toggling back-and-forth between whether or not I want to attempt to explain it or just say "It's just something that I always tell myself." I always land on the second option. I don't owe anyone an explanation just like that family friend didn't owe me one, even though I never asked him. Still, I know I wasn't entitled to an explanation. Honesty is faith could seem religious or to some, it could seem like a criticism of dishonesty. The truth is that it just means whatever I'm feeling at the moment. Being honest with myself has never been easy and I've realized that I don't need to have everything figured out or understand everything that bothers me.

I look around now, and I actively seek out tattoos on a person's body. My APCSP teacher had one on her back. As I stared at it in class, I kept on wondering what the significance between those tattoos were to her. It made sense to me that she had them; she was whimsical and let out a little giggle anytime she saw a cartoon animal. I don't know the significance behind her tattoos and it's important that I don't. They mean something to her that no one will ever be able to fathom. Making the decision to permanently place something onto your body is deeply intimate to yourself. You don't really owe anyone an explanation for what you decide to do with your body, meaning that no one's entitled to judging you. Following the winter recess, I entered the classroom with my new tattoo, and she noticed it. She didn't ask me what it meant, she didn't judge me, she told me she loved it. I felt a moment of solidarity. I felt relief when she didn't ask me what it meant because she too understood that it may be personal to some people.

Everytime I look at myself in the mirror, or look down, it's a reminder that my mom did the very thing that I would come to realize: It's about finding myself, it's about learning how others find themselves, through their lens, and finding yourself eventually as a result.
Connections and relationships aren't solely reliant on you and how you view things but it also doesn't mean you have to sacrifice your own views. My mother would never get one herself, but she didn't let her own values get in the way of mine.

I felt alone for the longest time thinking that I was the only one that didn't have not just life, but myself figured it out. But where I stand in the world isn't something that I want to master anymore. Just like I changed my mind about tattoos in the past years, I could wake up in 10 years with a fully developed frontal lobe and think "This is just not what I believe anymore" and I'm okay with not knowing if I will or not.

## New section: what changed her mind (her details, lightly shaped)

On the F train, I noticed a girl with long black hair down to her waist, like mine, and a fine-line word on her collarbone. I couldn't make out what it said. That's what I liked about it: only she knew. It felt like seeing myself in her, like she was what I'd always wanted to be. I just hadn't known what it was until then. I admired the confidence of doing something for yourself and not for the judgment of others.
